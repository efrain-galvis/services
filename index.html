What I take on

  1. 01

    Becoming an AI-driven company

    Further than automating a few processes with workflows. How the company works, decides, and builds when AI is part of the default way of operating — and which parts should stay human.

  2. 02

    Agents your users can talk to

    Putting your agents in front of the people who use your product, inside the app — not locked in an internal tool. Tool design, retrieval, memory, and the evaluation that makes them reliable enough to ship.

  3. 03

    AI-DLC — AI-driven development life cycle

    With the right tools, one person can cover what used to take a whole engineering team. I set up that way of working with you: where AI sits in each stage, and where a human still has to decide.

  4. 04

    Good practice in how you use AI

    Evaluation, guardrails, cost, and knowing how a system should fail. Also applied machine learning when a learned model is the right tool, rather than a language model by reflex.

  5. 05

    Reviews and pairing

    Short engagements to unstick a problem: review an approach, sit with your team, or help you choose what not to build.

How it goes

Small scope. Close loop. Something you can use.

No theater, no fake case studies. A straightforward way of working so you always know what we are doing and why.

  1. Step 01

    Tell me the problem

    The agent above is enough. What you are trying to ship, where it is stuck, and what good would look like.

  2. Step 02

    We agree on a useful slice

    A clear piece of work with a boundary: a prototype, a review, a feature, a set of evaluations. Not an open-ended retainer unless that is what you want.

  3. Step 03

    I work in the open with you

    You see the thinking as it happens — assumptions, dead ends, and the next decision. No disappearing for six weeks.

  4. Step 04

    You keep the work

    Code, notes, or a recommendation you can act on. The point is that your team can continue without me if that is the right call.
